# Private reading and content recovery

Reading Box collects for one owner. The SQLite service and the paired browser keep private reading copies on the owner’s devices. Metadata, article bodies and reading history have no public publishing endpoint. External AI calls remain disabled.

## Run it on Windows

1. In PowerShell, stop Reading Box with **Ctrl+C**. Back up the complete stopped service directory using the [Windows backup instructions](ARCHIVE_AND_PORTABLE_DATA.md#run-the-review-edition-on-windows), choosing a new folder name. Keep this copy private. This release upgrades SQLite from schema 4 to 5. An older service needs a working copy of its pre-upgrade database. Restoring that snapshot loses later service observations. Browser reading state has its own JSON backup.
2. From your existing clone with Node.js 24 run:

   ```powershell
   git fetch origin
   git switch feature/phase-2-private-content
   git pull --ff-only origin feature/phase-2-private-content
   npm.cmd ci
   npm.cmd run reading-box
   ```

3. Open the printed localhost launcher in the same Brave, Chrome or Edge profile, hostname and port. **Manage subscriptions → Recover article content** reassesses retained history and gives saved articles priority. Leave the terminal running. You can close the reader. The dialog shows remaining work, recoveries, partial results, restrictions and failures. Use **Check for deliveries** to update the browser immediately. New incomplete feed editions enter the queue automatically.
4. Save, archive and scroll an older publisher-only article before recovery. Reopen it from Saved or Archive after delivery. Check its Original label, final passage, copy action and reading position. Find a distinctive body word, then try both length sorts. Complete word counts drive the sort. Partial bodies show the available word count and remain in the unknown-full-length group.
5. Stop the service during recovery, reopen a previously delivered article offline, then restart the same command. A leased job resumes when its lease expires. Test a link-only blog, a full Atom feed and a Substack feed. Open a public preview at the publisher for the remainder. In **Library actions → Library backup** check a saved openly licensed article and an article without a portable-use grant. OPML continues to transfer subscriptions separately.

## How the service makes decisions

| Evidence                                                                                         | Private access and retention                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         | Additional uses                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Explicit full RSS `content:encoded` or Atom `content`, supported format and a substantive ending | Use the sanitised feed edition. Short ambiguous texts remain unverified. An ordinary description or Atom summary stays an excerpt. A preview marker, access wall or truncated edition takes precedence.                                                              | Preserve source, attribution, revision and the supplied licence. Unknown licence metadata permits ordinary personal reading under the source’s terms. It grants no JSON body export or external processing.                                                                                                                                                                  |
| Public article linked from the feed                                                              | Fetch without cookies or credentials. Prefer an identifiable article boundary. Otherwise run Mozilla Readability in LinkeDOM and compare the extracted ending with the page’s article or main element. If the match is uncertain, show a labelled available edition. | Copy permitted text with attribution. Export saved text when an explicit applicable licence allows it and its attribution can travel safely. Recognised Creative Commons grants and the Debian website’s MIT terms can support a private backup. CC0 can mark source text eligible for a future processor subject to applicable exclusions. There is no processor connected. |
| Subscriber-only, sign-in, robots denial, no-store, withdrawal or explicit exclusion              | Preserve permitted metadata and a separately syndicated public preview. Hand off to the publisher.                                                                                                                                                                   | No restricted body in search, full copying or backup. An offline device receives publisher changes at its next delivery.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| Technical failure                                                                                | Preserve the last permitted page after a temporary publisher outage. Retry eligible queue work with backoff and Retry-After; display its status.                                                                                                                     | A permanent failure or exhausted retries leaves the handoff available. A changed feed revision reassesses eligibility.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |

Per-entry declarations and publication-wide rights are evaluated independently of the presence of a body. A copyright notice or absence of a machine-readable licence alone does not block ordinary personal feed reading. Specific no-storage, copying and processing instructions take precedence. A licence covering the feed does not automatically grant reuse of material from a different page or a third-party illustration. Personal use is not a blanket copyright exemption. Substack’s supported public RSS endpoint supplies content for feed readers. Its terms prohibit scraping pages and substantial copying or storage of platform content. Reading Box uses the syndicated edition and hands an incomplete Substack post back to its publisher. [Substack RSS](https://support.substack.com/hc/en-us/articles/360038239391-Is-there-an-RSS-feed-for-my-publication) · [Platform terms](https://substack.com/tos) · [Debian licensing](https://www.debian.org/license)

Publishers can state terms outside their feed or machine-readable metadata. For additional known restrictions, put a `publisher-terms.json` file in the private service data directory and restart. For example, `{"https://publication.example":"feed-only","https://another.example":"no-retention"}` disables automated page collection for the first origin and body retention for the second. The service applies changed exclusions to existing editions and deliveries. A new public source needs no configuration or per-article approval.

The downloader validates public destinations and DNS at each hop, binds the connection to a validated address, checks robots, limits redirects, request time and response sizes, and never logs in. Pages run through an inert DOM and the structured content renderer. The reader preserves supported headings, links, tables, lists, block quotes, preformatted code and captions. Image downloads remain limited to explicitly licensed, validated, same-origin PNGs. Other images get a publisher link. This keeps third-party trackers out of the reader. A separately identified lead illustration can be linked alongside complete text when its publisher supplies an alternate-text attribute and its caption is preserved. Missing figures inside the article can still leave completeness unverified. Dynamic rendering, split articles and unrecognised paywalls or terms may limit completeness. The reason and publisher link remain visible.

Recovery runs in SQLite, one publisher task at a time. Jobs belong to the paired installation and source revision. Saved IDs are supplied by that browser when recovery is requested; other articles use publication date or first receipt. The queue records attempts, retry times and expiring leases. Reading data, article identity and pairing keep their existing storage keys. A removed subscription can be reassessed for its previously observed entries. New posts from that feed stay outside the library. Previously discarded text cannot be reconstructed if the publisher has also removed both feed and page. Each installation currently has a 64 MiB syndicated-body budget and a separate 16 MiB page budget. The service limits a response to 4 MiB and a structured article to 100,000 text characters. These limits bound memory, disk and publisher traffic. A full cache or an oversized edition can leave a publisher handoff.

## Availability measurement and device acceptance

Run `npm.cmd run test:availability` from a checkout with the merged Milestone 7 commit in its Git history and public internet access. The script runs the released Milestone 7 parser and this reader against the same feed bytes and reuses page responses within each pair. It measures every entry in the six feed windows. It also reports a balanced sample of up to 12 evenly spaced entries per feed. Both denominators include uncertain editions, missing pages and technical failures. Only explicit access or usage exclusions leave the eligible denominator. A technical failure can coexist with an available feed excerpt.
